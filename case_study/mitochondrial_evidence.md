## 3. Laboratory Findings — “Alcoholic Pattern” Without Alcohol

Despite **complete alcohol abstinence** (documented by urinalysis), labs have shown:

- **AST > ALT**
- Elevated **GGT**
- **Macrocytosis**
- Intermittent **elevated LDH/CK**
- Transaminases compatible with **steatosis / mitochondrial hepatopathy**

### Why Mitochondrial Disease Can Mimic “Alcoholic” Labs

- Mitochondrial **NADH/NAD⁺ imbalance** resembles the biochemical effects of ethanol metabolism.
- **β-oxidation failure** → microvesicular steatosis identical to alcoholic fatty liver.
- **GGT** rises with mitochondrial stress, not just alcohol.
- **AST** is enriched in mitochondria and rises in **muscle + hepatic mitochondrial injury**.
- Macrocytosis can reflect **impaired nucleic acid synthesis** and redox disturbance.
- Muscle involvement contributes to **AST/LDH/CK** elevations and can be misinterpreted as “alcoholic myopathy.”

With **negative ethanol testing** and lifelong abstinence, this pattern supports **mitochondrial hepatopathy / systemic mitochondrial dysfunction**, not alcohol use.

## 4. Neuropathy & Unilateral Atrophy in Mitochondrial Disease

**Why asymmetry is plausible in mitochondrial disease:**

- Many mitochondrial disorders are **heteroplasmic**.
- Mutant mtDNA load can vary **between limbs and muscle groups**.
- Different fibers/nerve branches carry different mitochondrial burdens.

This can produce:
- **Monomelic** or asymmetric mitochondrial atrophy
- Unilateral or patchy neuropathy
- Limb-specific weakness under load
- Side-specific cramping and collapse

**Patient pattern:**
- Lifelong **side-dominant weakness and atrophy**
- Documented limb asymmetry
- Sudden cramp and collapse in one calf under modest load
- Cardiopulmonary status preserved at the time of collapse

→ This fits published patterns of **asymmetric mitochondrial involvement** better than generalized neuropathy or deconditioning.
